The demo workflow

Everything below runs from python spike/demo.py --fast with no network. Twenty-one scripts in fixed order, each reading the files the previous ones wrote under spike/out/. Read once, then every downstream number is computed from geometry, never re-read.

Every published object carries the sheet, the printed value, the measured value, the residual, the rule that produced it, and an empty adjudication slot. In QGIS a click shows all of it. That is the "reviewer reaches provenance in seconds" promise.

The easements, settled

Tunnel easements 61985-1..4 are one drawn strip, not four figures. Loop 2 chained the 345 single-dash strokes on the rw_EASE_EXIST_align layer into eight trains. The strip has a north edge drawn under the R/W line, a south edge parallel to it, and closures at both ends. No stroke on the sheet divides it into four. The parcel bubbles' leaders go to R/W vertex circles, and the printed curve data sits on the R/W line itself.

Measured: the envelope is 48,410 sq ft against a table sum of 70,690. The two temporary construction easements overlap the two tunnel easements rather than tiling the strip. Their individual outlines live in the deeds, not on this sheet.

For the demo: segment 2 shows the two chains that close, the strip envelope with its area, and the four easements queued with this reason. That is the story: the system measures what the sheet draws and says what it cannot prove.

What has been built

Forty-four Python scripts, about 8,000 lines, all under spike/. Throwaway by design: they answer "does this work" and feed the demo. Grouped by role; pipeline scripts in the order demo.py runs them.

FileWhat it doesWrites
Pipeline, in run order
demo.pyRuns the 21 steps in order, timed, offline. Skips OCR and glyph reading when annotation text covers the sheet. Routes QGIS steps to QGIS's own Python.—
blocks.pyText blocks from vector glyph strokes: box and angle for every piece of lettering, no OCR.blocks.json
read_shx.pyText from AutoCAD SHX annotations, one block per annotation. The reader for Civil 3D 2016+ sheets. Confidence 1.0 by construction.read_shx.json
frame.pyWhere the map is on the sheet: map area vs furniture (tables, title block, legends), from the sheet itself.frame.json
alphabet.pyA sheet's own alphabet from its tables: the NO. column is a known sequence, so its glyphs become exemplars. Reads the line, curve and radial tables.alphabet.npz, tables.json
read_glyphs.pyFallback reader for stroked sheets without annotations: every block matched against the sheet alphabet. A glyph matches or is ?.read_glyph.json
ocr.pyFallback reader for scans: level each crop, RapidOCR, parse survey notation.read_rapid.json
solve.py + georef.pyOne solver over every printed control type: N/E callouts with leaders traced to their point, monument notes, grid ticks. Consensus fit to CCS83 zone 3, per-axis redundancy, refuses weak fits.georef.json
overlay.pyLinework to NAD83(2011) with the HTDP epoch shift, drawn on the LiDAR intensity image.sheet_linework.geojson
parcels.pyFaces from the heavy linework, named by parcel labels and their leaders, checked against the parcel table areas.parcels.geojson

Findings worth carrying forward. Annotation text is the reader for Civil 3D 2016+ sheets; the glyph alphabet stays for 2012-era sheets. CAD layers did not help association once reads were exact; they still separate furniture from map. The exception page's wrong-line group is the fastest diagnostic there is: render the measured line, look at twelve crops, fix the biggest cause, repeat. Two generic readers agreeing on hand lettering is a floor, not a method.
