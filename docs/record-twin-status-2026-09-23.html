Where we are

As of 2026-09-29 (day 19 of 26, six before the demo): the headline is reconstructability, not pass rate — how much of each sheet's drawn boundary a record course (a bearing and distance, a curve with a record direction, or a walk between two printed coordinates) rebuilds on its own, with no drawing shown. Six sheets, each foot counted once: 42.6% of drawn boundary (27,171 of 63,806 ft), 55.6% of the boundary the record actually dimensions (26,678 of 47,946 ft), 2 of 19 parcels closed against the official AREAS tables, 1.0% anchored on a printed coordinate. Four overnight loops (16–19, 09-27 to 09-29) got there and are told loop by loop below; the accuracy section further down has the full per-sheet table, what moved it, and what still limits it.

Two weeks of spikes proved the pieces first. The plan reset on 09-22 named the wall: not reading, but association, which drawn line a printed number describes. The 09-23 overnight loop attacked that on the Presidio set with the CAD structure hidden in the PDFs (layers and AutoCAD SHX text annotations) and roughly doubled the pass rates. A second loop the same afternoon made the bench deterministic, tripled the arc-length passes by splitting the long R/W curves where the drawing marks a boundary, and settled the tunnel-easement question: the sheet draws them as one strip, so the four are queued by design, not by failure. That pass-rate-and-coverage framing carried through loop 14 (see the timeline); loop 16 replaced it with reconstructability.

A third loop the same evening (18:00 to 21:10) took the test set from three sheets to six: the Presidio pair plus three sheets on the Marin approach of the Golden Gate Bridge (R-10741.1/.2/.3, 2017, Route 101, drafter CHaldenwang, a Civil 3D 2012-style export with no SHX annotations and no tables, read by OCR). A sixth loop the same night took the six accuracy items in order. A seventh loop, the next morning, tried the scan idea from gap 1 on R-65.2: cluster the glyph images of one hand, name each cluster once, read the rest by template. The clustering is clean (98.6 % purity on the keyed glyphs once glyphs are separated) and the naming works (57 of 61 agreed boxes vote, the vision model names 36 clusters from their contact strips with no disagreement), but the reader lands at 7 right, 0 wrong, 25 queued on the keyed 32 against the two-reader consensus's 16, 1, 15. What stops it is not recognition: hand digits touch and fuse in 197 of 273 boxes, hatch runs through text, and 8 of the 32 keyed boxes are detector boxes sitting on the wrong text before any reader runs. The next lever on scans is text-line detection on the binarised page.

An eighth loop, one leg, came from looking at one crop: the compound R/W curve on Presidio (R=1470.00', L=573.93'(T)) whose record is the table rows C15 and C16 with no drawn boundary between them. The run-sum rule existed for inline labels and for the small fillets, but the table-tag path grouped tags by the piece their leader landed on, and C15's leader lands on a radial tick. The tag path now uses the same parent-tagged arc pool as the inline checks and searches a tag's own curve for a window whose length matches: C16 passes as its two pieces (521.73 vs 521.76), C19 passes, C19's false radius fail is gone, and C21 is now measured on the right piece, where it still fails for real. Presidio row checks went 31 pass / 12 fail to 33 / 9 with nothing else moving on any sheet. C15 itself cannot pass alone: its drawn piece is C15 plus an untagged C18, and the (T) total that covers them already passes.

A ninth loop, one leg, followed the same crop. The thin alignment curve C21 points at is drawn as several PDF paths end to end, so curve paths whose ends meet with matching tangent and radius are now one parent; dash-dot patterns chain into trains like dashes; and a table's consecutive rows are checked as one run against the drawn run between the marks that exist, even when a row has no tag on the drawing. That last rule is what C15 needed: it passes as C15 + C16 + C18, the untagged neighbour, 573.95 against 573.93. C20 now reaches a check and fails honestly, since its leader lands on the alignment centreline. C21 is measured rather than fixed: its stitched parent reaches 706.79 of the record's 876.88 ft, and the next path, 10.65 pt away, is a different curve. Two things were tried and reverted with numbers: a wider join gained nothing on C21 and moved a neighbour's radius across tolerance, and dash-dot pieces reached by proximity alone produced six fake fails on R-10434.3, so they now need a leader.

A tenth, short loop traced C21 by eye with the checker's own candidate pool drawn on the sheet. It is all there: a tangent, then five pieces of one thin line across the C9/C10 fillet (255.91 + 193.93 + 86.86 + 278.14 + 62.04 = 876.88 ft), ending where three straight lines start along the curve's tangent, a point of tangency. Two rules stop the checker from seeing that: the join across the fillet compares a blended whole-piece radius (2.8 % off), and there is no point-of-tangency cut. Both fixes were tried and rejected on the no-loss gate, with numbers: radius fits on 40 ft windows swing from 1535 to 1382 ft at this sheet's two-decimal precision (100 ft is the floor), and a sheet-wide tangency cut fragments other curves and costs three passes elsewhere. The diff is kept; the scoped version is the next step. Nothing shipped; the loop 9 numbers stand.

An eleventh loop answered a question about the screen: why the purple road surface stops abruptly. Purple was the LiDAR vendor's bridge-deck class alone; it ends where the road leaves the structure, at the Battery tunnels (no return at all), where the class thins at parapets and joints, and on the 2018 3DEP tile, whose pavement came out as 190 fragments under the polygon floor. The class audit found ground on class 2 and deck on class 17 on every tile, so the fix was geometry and assembly, not classes: deck now grows from class-17 seeds into dark, flat cells two metres above the ground DTM; fragmented tiles get a wider closing; and one highway surface per tile dissolves pavement and deck inside the right-of-way faces, marked structure or at grade. The tunnel easements are drawn as record only, below ground. Gains in area are small and honest; the continuity on the screen comes from the union. Second, what share of the drawing is checked? Every value-shaped token on the three Presidio sheets now has a named fate (a table cell, a note, an area, a radial, a pass, a fail, a queue reason); none drops silently. Over the labels a check could address, 98 of 170 pass on R-10434.2, 60 of 168 on R-10434.1 and 115 of 246 on R-10434.3; that is the honest coverage figure and it is on the bench now. Coverage work then resolved tags with an unread glyph from the CAD text or from the one table row that passes its own check, and checked bare (T) run totals with the printed bearing required on the run: 16 new passes, none lost, every new crop viewed (one wrong-line pass was caught that way and the rule tightened). Overnight 09-27, a scan detector bake-off on the 1969 R-65.2 sheet (outside the six-sheet set) found PP-OCRv5's server detector lands 30–32 of 32 keyed boxes against Surya's 24 (which fuses R=/Δ/L stacks) and a vision model's 1; wired in opt-in behind the existing RapidOCR default. A fifteenth loop (09-27) fixed how a fail earns its keep. An arc fail now stands only when its association is proven — a lone candidate in the leader's reach, or a clear touch — otherwise it queues as unproven; that alone cut R-10434.3's fails 44 to 27 and its keyed-wrong count 24 to 7, all 19 real fails kept. A second cause of unfound labels turned out to be a box-rotation prefilter throwing out the right line whenever text was stacked; letting a bearing-agreeing candidate in added 12 passes, every one viewed. A gold set on R-10434.1 (83 right, 0 wrong, 7 real fails, 9 wrong-line, 4 unsure) joined Presidio's and R-10434.3's, so every keyed sheet read zero wrong-line passes.

Loop 16 (09-27 23:05 to 09-28), JR's ruling: accuracy means reconstructable. A foot of drawn boundary counts only when a record course walks it, unflagged, within 0.5 ft; a parcel counts only when every edge is walked by record and the figure closes within 1 ft — nothing about whether a label merely sits near a line. Measured cold, the six sheets rebuilt 29.5% of drawn boundary (20,876 of 70,661 ft), 38.9% of what the record actually dimensions, and 0 of 224 named parcels. Building the metric surfaced a triple-counting bug on the way: near-coincident copies of the same stroke (an R/W line drawn on top of a face edge) were being credited up to three times over; fixing it is why these numbers, and loop 17's, read lower than a naive read of the underlying passes would suggest, not higher. A ceiling measurement (leg M) found the honest limit: of the boundary these six sheets dimension anywhere, about 70% is reachable in principle; the rest is bearing-only, curves without a recorded direction, or already in honest disagreement with the drawing.

Loop 17 (09-28) found the single biggest lever. The R-10741 sheets' bearings were being read fine all along; what silently dropped them was text canonicalisation — OCR renders the compass letters lowercase and the degree/minute/second marks as ' * " instead of °′″, and the parser threw the whole bearing away instead of normalising it. Fixing that alone was the loop's biggest single gain, about five points of the set. Alongside it: traverse misfit moved from a cumulative judgement (one bad edge failed every edge chained after it) to one judged per edge, curve radii earlier passes had computed and then discarded now reach the labels that need them, and refused tags stopped leaking into the traverse as if they had passed. Set headline: 38.1% of drawn boundary, 49.0% of dimensioned boundary, first parcel closed (Presidio 61806-9).

Loop 18 (09-28 evening) taught the walk to use the record's own coordinate math. Per JR's ruling, a line between two printed coordinates is now a record course in its own right (an inverse), a “(T)” total counts for every piece of the one straight or curved line it labels, and a radial at a curve end counts only when the record circle it implies passes within 0.5 ft of every drawn point of that curve. R-10741.1's L=660.20' curve had been refused by a direction check that treated its two consistent end tangents as disagreeing (they differ by exactly 180°), and later lost the radius printed on its own label; both fixed. Curves gained two rules: two that happen to share a fillet no longer merge into one, and a curve that fails its own internal check is no longer walked as if it had passed.

Loop 19 (09-29), the coordinate-anchored rebuild queued since T-001. Starting at a printed coordinate on the boundary, the walker applies the record's own courses in order and accepts a chain only when it lands on the next printed coordinate within tolerance — a closure failure names the likely course, not just a mismatch. It closes: Presidio's four-course walk from one printed coordinate to the next lands 0.007 ft off (figure below). Anchored coverage is still small (1.0%, 660 ft) — few stretches of boundary run between two printed coordinates with nothing missing in between — but where it applies it is the strongest proof on the page: no drawing measured, only the record's own numbers walked to a value the drafter also printed. Cross-sheet chaining and closure-checked fills came back with nothing to show (0 joinable, 0 checkable) except one real find: DETAIL “A” on R-10741.2 closes parcel 46825-5, the second parcel of two. New gold sets on all three R-10741 sheets keyed every checked label; combined with Presidio's and R-10434's, that is 479 passes checked by eye across six sheets, 0 on the wrong line.

Tried and rejected the same day: loosening tolerance. JR asked what a full foot of slack (distance, bearing as a lateral offset, misfit, anchoring) would buy. Measured: +0.4 points of set boundary, all on R-10434.3, for 4 new wrong-line passes on the gold sets and 3 hidden real record disagreements. Not worth it; the defaults stand (distance 0.30 ft + 0.05%, bearing 0.05°, 0.5 ft rebuild bar).

The demo workflow

Everything below runs from python spike/demo.py --fast with no network, 129 s. A --six flag adds the other five sheets' tails and the six-sheet QGIS project, 640 s, 0 failures. Twenty-one scripts in fixed order, each reading the files the previous ones wrote under spike/out/. Read once, then every downstream number is computed from geometry, never re-read.

Every published object carries the sheet, the printed value, the measured value, the residual, the rule that produced it, and an empty adjudication slot. In QGIS a click shows all of it. That is the "reviewer reaches provenance in seconds" promise.

How much of the survey is reconstructable

JR's definition, adopted 2026-09-27: a foot of drawn boundary counts only when a record course — a bearing and distance, or a curve with a record chord direction — walks it, unflagged, within 0.5 ft of the drawing; a parcel counts only when every edge is walked by record and the figure closes within 1 ft. Nothing about whether a label happens to sit near a line. Six sheets, each foot of ground counted once (where two R-10741 sheets draw the same ground, it is counted once): 42.6% of drawn boundary rebuilt from record alone (27,171 of 63,806 ft), 55.6% of the boundary the record actually dimensions (26,678 of 47,946 ft), 2 of 19 parcels closed against the official AREAS tables (61806-9, within 0.1% of the record area; 46825-5 via DETAIL "A"), and 1.0% (660 ft) anchored — rebuilt stretches that also land on a printed coordinate within about 0.01 ft.

History: at the start of loop 16 (2026-09-27) this measure read 29.5% drawn / 38.9% dimensioned / 0 parcels. That early figure summed each sheet's boundary separately and double-counted the R-10741 tile overlap; on that same (uncorrected) basis, today's number would read 43.2%.

Rebuilt ft = drawn boundary a record course walks unflagged. Labels checked = drawing labels a check can address that pass (distance + bearing + arc, over addressable labels). Gold = every checked label keyed by eye against a crop; wrong-line = the reader measured a different line than the label describes.

Zero wrong-line gold passes on all six gold-keyed sheets (479 passes keyed right in total). The R-10741 gold sets are new this loop (loop 19 leg 5) — those three sheets hold about 62% of the rebuilt footage, so they were the biggest hole in the precision claim until now. The 39 fails left across the six sheets are genuine record-vs-drawing disagreements, not reading or association mistakes: for example R-10741.1 prints S10°30'18"E 371.76' where the sheet draws 364.61 ft.

What moved it (loops 16–19)

Measurement made honest first. Each line is now judged on its own, where one bad line used to fail every correct line chained after it on the same run. Duplicate strokes (an R/W line drawn on top of a face edge) are now counted once — they were being credited up to 3×. Non-boundary linework is out of the denominator. The overlap between the R-10741 sheets (where .1/.2 and .2/.3 draw the same ground) is counted once across the set, not once per sheet. Parcels are scored against the official AREAS tables, not against every named face on the drawing.

Then reading and association. The single biggest gain: the north sheets' OCR renders bearings with lowercase compass letters and ' * " in place of the degree mark, and the parser was silently dropping the whole bearing rather than normalising it — worth about +5 points alone once fixed. Also: labels on stubs now resolve; refused table rows that had been leaking into the traverse are excluded; curve radii that earlier passes computed and then discarded now reach the labels that need them; radial capture rose from 43 to 98 curve ends; curves that had wrongly merged across a shared fillet split back apart; a curve that fails its own check is no longer walked as if it had passed; R-10741.1's L=660.20' curve is rebuilt again (a 180° direction-check bug, then its own printed radius bound to the wrong piece).

Then record anchors. A “(T)” total now counts as a whole course for every piece of the one straight or curved line it labels (860.77 ft(T) is one course, not several unlabelled pieces). DETAIL “A” closes 46825-5. And walking the record between two printed coordinates, where it applies: 8 chains close within about 0.01 ft, none fail.

What limits it

Share of the dimensioned boundary still not rebuilt, by cause: 62% has no record course reaching the line at all (52 points of that is unlabelled — much of it only referenced elsewhere, “SEE R-…” or a deed, or never dimensioned anywhere in this set); 17% is bearing-only lines (the distance is missing); 12% is curves with no recorded direction; 5% is distance-only lines (the bearing is missing); 4% is a genuine record/drawing disagreement. The ceiling, measured at the start of loop 18 with duplicates removed: about 85% of the dimensioned boundary is reachable in principle. That is the honest limit of these six sheets, not of the method.

Tried and not kept

Measured and set aside: a stronger OCR detector on R-10741 scored 6 of 14 keyed boxes against RapidOCR's 14 of 14, so no change; PDF draw order, grouping and label-placement rules never beat nearest-line association; curve-to-curve chaining completed no curve; coordinate inverses between labelled points, 0 qualify; cross-sheet chains, 0 joinable; closure-checked fills, 0 checkable. The coordinate-anchored rebuild (loop 19, “step four”) works as an independent check but adds little coverage on its own — few stretches run between two printed coordinates with nothing missing in between.

Rejected on the gate: JR asked what loosening every tolerance to a full foot would buy (distance, bearing as a lateral offset, misfit, anchoring). Measured: +0.4 points of set boundary, all on R-10434.3, for 4 new wrong-line gold passes and 3 hidden real record disagreements. Not worth it — the defaults stand: distance 0.30 ft + 0.05%, bearing 0.05°, 0.5 ft rebuild bar.

Next: a coordinate-table value reader for bearing-only lines (the distance is on the sheet, just not beside the bearing); curves still missing a recorded direction; a general detail-inset reader (DETAIL “A” was read by hand this loop); bring the 1969 scans into the score.

The easements, settled

Tunnel easements 61985-1..4 are one drawn strip, not four figures. Loop 2 chained the 345 single-dash strokes on the rw_EASE_EXIST_align layer into eight trains. The strip has a north edge drawn under the R/W line, a south edge parallel to it, and closures at both ends. No stroke on the sheet divides it into four. The parcel bubbles' leaders go to R/W vertex circles, and the printed curve data sits on the R/W line itself.

Measured: the envelope is 48,410 sq ft against a table sum of 70,690. The two temporary construction easements overlap the two tunnel easements rather than tiling the strip. Their individual outlines live in the deeds, not on this sheet.

For the demo: segment 2 shows the two chains that close, the strip envelope with its area, and the four easements queued with this reason. That is the story: the system measures what the sheet draws and says what it cannot prove.

The north set

CHaldenwang's callouts read differently from the Presidio drafter's. Coordinates print as a two-row block, N over a bar over E, with a thin (< 1.0 pt) leader running to a filled arrowhead that sits 10.6–10.9 pt short of a small circle rather than touching it. Civil 3D 2012-style export: no SHX text annotations, no tables, so every block is read by OCR. Three callouts paired on R-10741.1, four on .2, three on .3 (10 of 10 traced) once the leader trace learned two rules: walk across thin weights (below 1.0 pt) instead of stopping at them, and snap to a circle within 12 pt of the arrowhead when no second circle sits within 24 pt.

Placement: sheet-derived fits (scale and rotation from the sheet's own bearings and distances, offset from the traced callouts) land at rms 0.04 / 0.05 / 0.03 ft on .1/.2/.3. Checked against Caltrans' package at 9 sheet points: max 0.29 / 0.37 / 0.40 ft. .2 is credible outright; .1 and .3 are weaker fits (3 callouts each) with the rotation confirmed independently by 12 printed bearings apiece. Sheet-derived scale agrees with the package to 0.011 % and rotation to 0.0051°. The package is now the check on all six sheets and the offset on none.
