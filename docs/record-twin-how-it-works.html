Record Twin Field Guide

A right-of-way record map goes in. A checked, georeferenced object record comes out, on the LiDAR, with a queue of everything the system could not prove. This page teaches the machine: each section is one load-bearing mechanism, drawn, with what breaks when it is wrong.

SECTION 1The problem, in one picture

Caltrans District 4 holds thousands of record maps: legal drawings of what the state owns along a highway. Each sheet carries the record twice. Once as drawn geometry (lines, curves, circles at vertices), and once as printed numbers (bearings, distances, arc lengths, coordinates, areas). Nobody has ever checked one against the other at scale, and nobody has put either on the ground.

Load-bearing: the queue is a product, not a failure log. A pass that the drawing cannot support is worse than a queued item, because a wrong "verified" stamp on a right-of-way line is the one outcome the project must never produce.

SECTION 2Anatomy of a sheet: where the numbers live

Every mechanism downstream depends on knowing which kind of number it is looking at. A distance in the line table is not a label on the drawing; a distance inside a not-to-scale detail bubble can never match anything; a coordinate callout is control, not a check.

Load-bearing: masks. The map area is separated from furniture (tables, title block, legend) by the sheet's own geometry, and table cells are masked before any label check runs. Loop 4 found twelve table cells on R-10434.3 being checked as drawing labels because a mask stopped short; that alone was a third of that sheet's wrong-line count.

SECTION 3The pipeline: what writes what

One command, offline, twenty-one scripts in a fixed order. Each script reads the files the earlier ones wrote under spike/out/ and writes its own. The order is the dependency graph: nothing downstream re-reads the PDF's text, and nothing upstream knows about the LiDAR.

Load-bearing: a file with two writers is a moving baseline. The two state bugs found in loop 3 (who writes tables.json last; a reads selector that took a 22-byte file as a validated alphabet) had shifted whole sheets' counts by tens without any logic change. Every bench since runs the canonical order.

SECTION 4Reading text: three readers, one rule

Sheets come in three classes, and each gets the reader that is exact for it. The selector is a rule, not a score: a sheet reads by annotation where it has annotations, by its own glyph alphabet where its tables can validate that alphabet, and by OCR otherwise.

How the glyph alphabet works

Stroked text has no characters, only paths. The trick is that a sheet's tables have a NO. column with a known sequence (L1, L2, L3…; C1, C2…). A public stroke font finds those cells, the consecutive run proves they are what they look like, and their glyphs become exact exemplars of this drafter's hand. Every other glyph on the sheet is then matched by 24 px bitmap against those exemplars at 36 rotations. A glyph matches or it is ?; nothing reads by confidence.

Load-bearing: the reads decide the denominators of every number downstream. A label that does not read is not a fail, it is invisible; that is why the guide reports pass rates over three denominators (section 7), and why the north sheets read by OCR score high on what they check and low on what they see.

SECTION 5Placing the sheet on the earth

Placing means one similarity transform: sheet points to ground coordinates, with scale, rotation and offset. Four things can fix it, in order of how much they prove.

Load-bearing: refusal. A four-point fit with 0.3 ft residuals can still be wrong by 13 % in scale; the solver refuses rather than reports, and every fit prints its disagreement with the package. Two scan fits that looked fine were caught by the grid-label scale check.
Tiles. Three LiDAR tiles are on disk (Presidio 2025, Marin 2025, a 3DEP 2018 tile for the gap between them). tiles.py maps a sheet to its tile by name, and the ground steps key everything off that. The epoch shift is one number for the site, from a real NGS HTDP call, cached so the demo never needs the network.

SECTION 6Association: which line does this number describe?

This is the wall. Reading is exact on the good sheets and placing is solved; what is hard is deciding, for a printed 138.21', which of the fifty drawn pieces near it is the one it describes. Every wrong-line exception in the project is this decision made badly. The mechanism is a candidate pool, then filters that each remove a class of wrong answers.

The diagnostic loop that built it

Every association rule in the codebase came from the same procedure: render the twelve largest failures with the label in red and the measured line in blue, name the cause of each, count the causes, fix the biggest, re-bench, repeat. It is cheap, it is honest, and it stops the temptation to tune thresholds by feel. Two of loop 6's findings came from crops alone: the "chord citation" was a dashed line whose dashes were 8.6 pt against an 8 pt cap, and cut pieces were inheriting their chain's seed direction.

Load-bearing: the tolerance is the drawing's, not ours. A piece 20 pt long cannot resolve a bearing better than about 2°, so the bearing tolerance scales with piece length and is capped, and pieces under 10 pt are queued as too short. Without the cap, a formula that looked principled manufactured thirty passes in one leg; the cap was set from the crop captions, not from the pass count.

SECTION 7Verdicts, the queue, and three honest denominators

Every checked label ends in one of four states, and the state names the reason. The exception page groups the non-passes by cause with a crop, a citation in PDF points, and a resolution field a surveyor fills in.

StateMeaningWhere it goes
passprinted agrees with drawn under the fit, within the drawing's own tolerance; or "pass as a run of N" where several tags share one drawn runobject record, verified

Load-bearing: a pass set, not a pass count. Every leg since loop 4 is gated on a per-label diff of which labels pass, so a change that adds three passes and silently drops three others cannot hide inside an unchanged total. Denominators are allowed to shrink only when a class of label is shown to be uncheckable, and the reason is recorded.

SECTION 8Walking the record: the traverse

The checks say "this label agrees with this line". The traverse asks the deeper question: do the printed numbers, walked in order, come back to where they started? That is what a surveyor's closure is, and it needs no label-to-line heuristic at all once the edges are placed.

The tunnel easements, settled. Parcels 61985-1..4 were expected to close as four figures. The sheet draws them as one dashed strip (345 strokes chained into eight trains), with no stroke dividing it and the curve data sitting on the R/W line. Envelope 48,410 sq ft against a table sum of 70,690: the temporary easements overlap the tunnel ones. Their outlines live in the deeds. The system measures what the sheet draws and says what it cannot prove.

SECTION 9Ground truth from the LiDAR

Once a sheet is placed and shifted to the LiDAR epoch, its faces and lines sit on real returns. Airborne density supports three things and no more: pavement (dark, flat ground returns), viaduct deck (the bridge-deck class), and building footprints (building class with a flat top). Every feature is a raster decision on a 1 m grid then polygonised, so every polygon can be traced to the rule and the returns that made it.

SECTION 10Measuring honestly: the bench and the gates

The project's second product is its discipline. Every change is a "leg" with a gate written before the work, measured by an orchestrator who did not write the code, on a bench whose procedure is fixed. A leg that fails its gate twice is stashed, not forced. The status table in spike/LOOP.md records every leg, its numbers, and what was accepted on override and why.

Load-bearing: the baseline row. spike/out/bench.csv keeps one row per sheet per leg; the current baseline is loop6-final. Any number quoted anywhere in the project is re-measured from a row at report time or marked unverified.

SECTION 12Glossary

record map
A right-of-way sheet: the legal drawing of what the state owns, with the survey record printed on it.
bearing · distance
A line's direction (N67°37'21"W: 67°37'21" west of north) and length in US survey feet. Printed beside the line or in a table row.
curve data
R (radius), Δ (central angle), L (arc length) for a curve. L = R·Δ is an internal check that needs no drawing.
(T)
A total: the printed length of a whole run that several tags share. Checked as a run-sum.
callout
A printed N/E coordinate with a leader to the point it locates. The sheet's own control.
leader · arrowhead
The thin line from a label to the thing it describes, ending at a filled triangle or a vertex circle.
SHX annotation
AutoCAD's text as an annotation object in the PDF. Exact text, exact rectangle. Present on Civil 3D 2016+ exports.
glyph alphabet
Per-sheet exemplars of the drafter's stroked characters, seeded by a public font and validated by the tables' NO. column.
CCS83 zone 3
California Coordinate System, NAD83, zone 3, US survey feet: what the sheets print. Converted to UTM 10N metres for the LiDAR.
HTDP
NGS's Horizontal Time-Dependent Positioning: the epoch shift between the sheet's datum epoch and the LiDAR's. 0.668 m toward N33°W here.
package
Caltrans' own georeferenced raster of a sheet (tif + tfw) from the D4 index. Used as a check on the fit, never as an input.
wrong-line
An exception whose disagreement is far too large to be a drafting error: the reader measured a different line. The main diagnostic.
canonical bench
The fixed procedure that produces a bench row: annotation tables rebuilt, then checks, on all six sheets, per-label pass sets diffed.
leg · gate · loop
One change, its acceptance criterion written first, and the sequence of legs run overnight or in an evening with an orchestrator re-measuring each.
tile
One LiDAR delivery: points.laz + DEM. Three on disk; a sheet is routed to its tile by name.
